I frequently find myself thinking about various scenes from BBC's nature documentaries, especially in times of hardship. 

It's been years since I watched them. I can't help but think of the cape gannets.[^1] Their chicks hatch on an Island and they are left to fend for themselves against cape seals. Food scarcity from overfishing makes it so their parents have to travel miles to find schools of sardines to feed them. The chick receives only a single feeding, until it inherits the burden to do the same. Like most seabirds, gannets fledge independently. After the first feeding, the chick weighs more than its parents. It must begin fast to slim down, so it can weigh less, to be able to learn to fly. Subsequent feedings would only delay the path to self-sufficiency. For most seabirds, fledging ends the familial bond and parental care. 

![gf](https://www.africanbirdclub.org/afbid/public/imgdata/photos/26/261123220887.jpg)

The other scene I think of, is the one with the Marine iguana hatchlings on the Galápagos. As soon as they are born, they are ambushed and chased en masse by racer snakes from rocks surrounding their nest. They have to outsmart them and dash into the water to survive.[^2] The last one I think of is the barnacle goose chicks that are born and have to jump off of a cliff. Barnacle goose goslings leap more than 400 ft from cliff nests to reach their parents below; many tumble and some perish, but survivors reunite quickly so the family can move on before predators arrive. This extreme descent is essential to reach feeding grounds soon after hatching.[^3]
There are other scenes too, and different ones will resonate depending on which season of life you are currently weathering at the moment. The resonance comes from wanting to project your life experience onto what other creatures on earth go through and relate. I find a deeper empathy behind the plight of animals sometimes rather than media about the human experience. 

I'm a new parent. I think about the brevity of the familial bonds of seabirds. I learn to cherish the mundanity of routine tasks of caring for my daughter; her stubbornness in not wanting to go to bed, or wanting me to help her walk around and explore for the tenth time today. It is all time I'm grateful for. I think about the iguana and goose chicks. I read that [my job as a parent is to make my kids lives a little harder](
https://thewalrus.ca/my-job-as-a-parent-is-to-make-my-kids-lives-a-little-harder/). I reflect on my own upbringing, for better or for worse. I'm grateful that my parents pushed me to hit certain checkpoints and overcome hurdles. I have a greater appreciation for maintain a certain tempo to my life and overcoming stasis. I no longer wonder why cape gannets do not get a second feeding. 

[^1] From *Parenthood*
[^2] From *Planet Earth II*
[^3] From *Life Story*
